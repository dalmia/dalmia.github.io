3 papers accepted to GlobalSouth AI at NeurIPS

It is refreshing to see many AI conferences prioritising deployment papers in the last couple of years and I am happy to share 3 papers accepted to GlobalSouth AI at NeurIPS this year. I will be in Sydney and would love to meet you if you are interested in AI for social impact/AI evaluation/AI safety/AI for Global South too!

1. Model selection for Hindi form-filling voice agents under noisy speech, cost and latency constraints

With Sanskriti and Jigar, in collaboration with ARMMAN

We share how we evaluated a Hindi form-filling voice agent for enrolling pregnant mothers in rural India without deploying to real users, using simulated calls built from our pilot failures. We test each component in isolation and evaluate the agent end-to-end by chaining the components, showing how errors in speech recognition on real-world audio by frontier models degrade the downstream task completion, and share how we pick models trading off latency, cost and accuracy.

2. Auditable Emergency Triage for Maternal and Newborn Care in India

With Shobhit, Dr. Niharika, Anubhav and Noora Health team members

Noora Health runs a WhatsApp service where nurses answer over 50,000 medical queries per month, including emergency messages. Identifying these emergency messages is not straightforward and using a purely LLM-based approach made it hard for domain experts to audit mistakes and iterate. So, we decomposed triaging into two steps: an LLM that extracts symptoms and patient context from the query against a clinician-authored vocabulary, and a deterministic rule engine that captures the scenarios that indicate an emergency. Not only does the new system improve performance, it makes the entire pipeline easily auditable by domain experts which resulted in a much faster error correction loop.

3. Evaluating the Early Deployment of a GenAI Co-Pilot for Frontline Maternal and Newborn Health Communication: A Mixed-Methods Case Study from India

With Zezhen Wu, PhD and Noora Health team members

A case study evaluating how Noora Health's multilingual AI co-pilot for nurses affects their agency, communication practices, and message quality in maternal and newborn health services in India.
